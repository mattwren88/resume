About

I'm Matt. I build things because I want them to exist. Right now that means a map of every historical marker in Pennsylvania, a calendar of what's going on around Scranton, and a hundred years of one street corner on a slider.

For work, I build and fix websites, mostly inside content management systems and mostly for colleges. I've been at it since 2011, long enough to watch the same three ideas come back around twice.

I started in radio, running digital for a handful of stations out of Pittston. Then eleven years at the University of Scranton, where I built their digital publication from nothing and learned what it actually takes to move a page through a large organization.

Scranton is also where I met Cascade. The university ran on it, so I learned it as a customer first, which I still think is the best way to learn a CMS. I spoke at Hannon Hill's user conference twice while I was there and got to know the people building it, and after a while going to work with them seemed like the obvious move. Three years in now, I sit between Support and Professional Services. Usually I'm who you get when the problem doesn't fit in a ticket, which is my favorite kind of problem. In 2026 I got to speak at that conference a third time, this time as staff, walking a room through building a Cascade site from scratch.

The through-line, if there is one: I try to hand things off. A component nobody can edit without calling me isn't finished. The documentation is as much the deliverable as the code — I've written enough of it that non-technical editors build their own pages and never file a ticket at all.

Away from work I make things about Pennsylvania. A fair amount of it is tedious, honestly. Long evenings copying rows out of one spreadsheet and into another. But I like knowing the answer at the end of it, and I like handing it to someone else for free. Scranton is a more interesting place than it gets credit for, and all three are basically arguments for that.
